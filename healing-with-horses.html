Why Horses, Specifically

The horses hold space

As herd animals and prey animals, horses’ survival has always depended on reading the truth of what’s around them — not what’s said, but what’s felt. That makes them extraordinarily honest company. A horse doesn’t respond to the version of you that’s holding it together; it responds to what’s actually moving underneath.

This is the beginning of something researchers call co-regulation — one nervous system finding its way toward calm in the presence of another that’s already there. It’s still a young field of study, and we hold that honestly rather than overselling it. But what we witness, week after week, is people meeting a 500-kilogram animal that has absolutely no interest in their job title, their diagnosis, or their bad morning — only in whether they’re safe to be near. For many people, that’s the first honest mirror they’ve had in a long time.

There’s no single way to be with the horses here. Some days it’s simply a pat over the fence and a quiet chat — connection, nothing more required. Other days it’s a wordless hour standing together, letting non-verbal communication do the talking. For some, it becomes a full somatic session, working with what the body’s been carrying. And for others, the horses’ calm, steady presence sits alongside reiki, amplifying the energy work already moving through the body. However you arrive, the benefits meet you there — a slower heart rate, a steadier breath, more capacity, and a nervous system quietly remembering what safety feels like.

Somatic

Equine Assisted Therapy

$150 · 60 minutes · one-on-one

This isn’t riding, and it isn’t talk therapy. It’s ground work — slow, deliberate time in the horse’s space, guided by a practitioner trained to read what’s happening in both bodies, human and horse. Somatic work starts from a simple premise: the story lives in the body before it ever reaches words. Alongside the horse, you’re invited to notice — breath, posture, the urge to step back or move closer — and to let the horse’s response become information rather than judgement. It’s gentle, it’s unhurried, and it meets you exactly where your nervous system is, not where you think it should be.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need any experience with horses, or riding skills?

None at all. This isn't about riding — it's about being present with the horses on the ground, and that's a skill they'll help you find, not one you need to arrive with.

Will I actually be riding?

No — the work happens on the ground, in the horses' space, not on their backs.

What if I'm nervous around horses, or I've never been close to one?

That's more common than not, and it's welcome here. Horses tend to meet nervous energy with patience rather than judgment — often, the nerves settle faster than people expect once they're actually standing in the paddock.

What actually happens during a session?

We always begin with a grounding outside the horse yard — opening the senses, calming the mind, settling the nervous system. Once you're regulated, the horses sense it, and that's when they let you into their space. From there, it's your time: you might simply pat, brush, and connect with the horses through non-verbal communication, or move into a somatic therapy session, where we talk through whatever you're carrying.

This isn't traditional talk therapy. We don't go into timelines or details — we work with how the body reacts, not the words. We can't change the past. What we can work with is what's been left behind: the discordant energy that activates when something's triggered. The horses amplify that movement, that shift. It's something you need to feel to truly understand.

Is this "therapy" in a clinical sense, or more of a guided experience?

My training is somatic-based. This isn't traditional talk therapy — it's a guided, embodied experience where the horses and your body do the work that words often can't reach.

How many horses do you work with, and does someone meet the same horse each time?

We work with a herd of six horses. Which horse shows up for you can shift from session to session — that's part of the work, not a detail to control.

Is it safe? What happens if a horse doesn't want to engage?

Yes — sessions are ground-based and practitioner-guided. Throughout, my role is to keep you safe, to gently guide you back into your body if things get intense, and to monitor how your nervous system is responding the whole way through. If a horse chooses not to engage, that's not a failure of the session — a horse's boundary often mirrors something worth sitting with, and it becomes part of the work itself.

Can I join if I have a physical limitation or mobility issue?

Yes — the property is wheelchair accessible.

What should I wear or bring?

Closed-toe shoes and comfortable, weather-appropriate clothing — you'll be standing on paddock ground, so layers are a good idea whatever the season.

Is it one-on-one, group, or both?

Both. You can book a private Equine Assisted Therapy or Energy Healing session, or join a group offering like a Fire Horse Session.
